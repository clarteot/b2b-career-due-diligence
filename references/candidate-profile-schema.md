# Candidate Profile Schema

Use this schema when a resume or work history is available.

## A. Career baseline
- total relevant experience
- current / recent role
- career progression
- individual contributor vs manager

## B. Industry and product context
- industries served
- SaaS / software / services / hardware / hybrid
- product complexity
- regulated or high-compliance experience

## C. Customer complexity
- SMB / mid-market / enterprise / strategic accounts
- domestic / global customers
- stakeholder seniority
- account portfolio size
- deal / contract scale when disclosed

## D. Commercial ownership
Identify actual ownership of:
- new-logo sales
- renewal
- expansion / upsell
- pricing / negotiation
- forecasting / quota

Do not infer commercial ownership from “responsible for customer relationship”.

## E. Discovery and consulting capability
Look for evidence of:
- customer interviews
- business-process mapping
- root-cause analysis
- use-case discovery
- requirements prioritization
- ROI / business-case construction

## F. Solution capability
Look for:
- solution design
- product configuration
- integration planning
- POC design
- standard vs customized solution tradeoffs

## G. Delivery capability
Look for:
- onboarding
- implementation
- project plan
- UAT / acceptance
- rollout
- issue escalation
- cross-functional coordination

## H. Adoption and value realization
Look for:
- training
- workshops
- usage growth
- change management
- executive business reviews
- value measurement

## I. Product influence
Look for:
- requirement translation
- product feedback
- roadmap influence
- bug / issue validation
- multi-customer pattern recognition

## J. Results evidence
Prefer quantified proof:
- revenue
- renewal
- expansion
- adoption
- customer retention
- time saved
- cost reduced
- project delivery result

If no metric exists, preserve qualitative evidence instead of inventing numbers.

## K. Case library
Extract 3–5 cases. For each:
- context
- business problem
- candidate action
- internal / external stakeholders
- outcome
- capabilities proven

Tag each case, e.g.:
- discovery
- solution design
- project recovery
- product influence
- executive relationship
- renewal
- expansion
- adoption
- negotiation

## L. Transferable capabilities
Identify capabilities likely to survive a change in product or industry.

Examples:
- complex discovery
- executive communication
- cross-functional execution
- solution design
- stakeholder mapping
- value realization

## M. Gaps
Separate:
- domain knowledge gap
- product / technology gap
- scale gap
- customer-segment gap
- management gap
- commercial-ownership gap
- evidence gap

## N. Preferences
Only use explicit user statements.

Possible dimensions:
- desired role
- responsibilities to avoid
- industry interest
- compensation
- location
- travel
- work intensity
- management vs IC
- new-logo quota tolerance

Never infer preference from past job history alone.
