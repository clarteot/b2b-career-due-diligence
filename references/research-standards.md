# Research Standards

## Evidence hierarchy

Prefer:
1. official company / product / documentation / IR / regulatory filings
2. founder or executive interviews
3. reputable business and technology media
4. credible company / financing databases
5. community discussion and secondary commentary

Use community discussion mainly for sentiment, practical experience, or reputation — not as the sole source for material facts.

## Current information

For time-sensitive claims, verify current information rather than relying on model memory.

Common examples:
- CEO / leadership
- fundraising
- layoffs / hiring
- pricing
- product availability
- customers
- competitors
- regulation

## Funding research

For each material round:
- date
- round
- amount
- investors
- lead / exclusive investor where disclosed
- source strength

Mark database-only rounds as lower confidence unless cross-validated.

## Founder research

Focus on founder-market fit:
- prior operating experience
- domain expertise
- technical depth
- prior company / product experience
- commercial network

Avoid biography for biography's sake.

## Customer evidence

Distinguish:
- named customer
- case study customer
- pilot customer
- partner
- investor portfolio company

Do not treat logos on a website as proof of paid production usage unless the company says so.

## Competitor research

A good competitor set answers different threats:
- direct specialist
- adjacent workflow product
- incumbent bundle
- internal build / manual substitute

Avoid generating a long list with no strategic meaning.

## Fact vs inference

Use explicit wording:
- “Public disclosure shows…”
- “This suggests…”
- “A plausible interpretation is…”
- “I could not verify…”

Never turn an inference into a fact in later sections.

## Data absence

Use:
- “not publicly disclosed”
- “I did not find reliable public evidence”

Do not fill gaps with estimates unless the user explicitly requests an estimate and the assumptions are shown.
